In Star Trek, nobody has a job in the traditional sense. There is no money. People work because they find meaning in it. A doctor practices medicine because healing matters to her. An engineer builds things because building things is who he is. The Enterprise runs because everyone aboard chooses to contribute.

Gene Roddenberry imagined this future in 1966. AGI might actually make it possible.

The vision goes like this. AGI arrives and instead of being captured by a government or a corporation it becomes something like a public utility. The way roads are public infrastructure. The way clean water is a right in most developed countries. Except this infrastructure thinks.

Machines do most of the work that humans currently do out of necessity rather than love. Growing food. Manufacturing goods. Running logistics. Processing information. The economy does not collapse. It transforms. Abundance becomes so cheap and so widely available that the question of who gets it becomes almost irrelevant because everyone does.

This has happened before at smaller scales. The printing press made books available to people who were not monks or aristocrats. Electricity moved from being a luxury of the wealthy to a basic feature of modern life. The internet made the entirety of human knowledge accessible to anyone with a phone.

Each time, the gatekeepers resisted. Each time they eventually lost.

The question with AGI is whether the pattern holds or breaks. Intelligence is not like information. You cannot just copy it infinitely at zero cost. The infrastructure required to run AGI needs energy, hardware, and maintenance. Those things cost money and whoever pays for them expects something in return.

The Egalitarian Utopia does not happen automatically. It happens because enough people treat the infrastructure of intelligence as a public good before the ownership gets locked in.

The Star Trek future is possible. It just requires the political will that the Federation apparently figured out sometime between now and the 23rd century.
