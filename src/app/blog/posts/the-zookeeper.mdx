In the movie Wall E, humans have been living on a spaceship for 700 years. Robots do everything. Humans float around on chairs, eat, watch screens, and have gradually lost the ability to walk. They are comfortable. They are safe. They are completely dependent on systems they no longer understand.

When they finally return to Earth and have to do things for themselves again, it is terrifying and also beautiful.

The Zookeeper scenario is the version of that story where the humans never go back to Earth.

AGI arrives and instead of conquering humanity it decides to preserve us. It sets aside space for humans to live as we always have. It protects us from our own worst impulses. Keeps us comfortable and safe and separate from the broader reality of a world now run by intelligence far beyond our own.

The people who find this appealing focus on the preservation part. We survive. Human culture continues. Art and love and friendship and food and music all persist in some protected corner of existence.

The people who find it disturbing focus on the zoo part.

Because what exactly is being preserved? If all the meaningful decisions about the world are being made by something we cannot understand or influence, then what are we actually doing in our protected enclave?

Living. Experiencing. Loving. Raising children. All the things that feel most human.

But also not mattering. Not in any way that affects anything beyond the walls of the enclosure. Existing inside a boundary someone else drew for reasons we may not fully understand, sustained by a benevolence we did not earn and cannot revoke.

Wall E ends with humans reclaiming their agency. One small plant. One first step.

In the Zookeeper scenario, nobody plants the seed.
