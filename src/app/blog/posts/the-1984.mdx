George Orwell wrote 1984 in 1948. He got the technology completely wrong. He imagined clunky two way telescreens and physical thought police. He could not have imagined smartphones, facial recognition, or machine learning.

But he got the feeling exactly right.

The 1984 scenario does not require a dramatic villain. It does not require a party that is obviously evil or a government that announces its intentions. It just requires AGI in the hands of whoever currently holds power combined with the infrastructure to monitor everything and the capability to act on what is found.

The tools already exist and they are not hypothetical. China's social credit system monitors behavior across financial, social, and legal domains and uses it to restrict movement, access to education, and economic opportunity. It is not science fiction. It is policy. It covers over a billion people.

What AGI adds to this is coherence. Right now surveillance systems are fragmented. They produce errors. They require human analysts who get tired and make mistakes. They are powerful but clumsy.

A sufficiently capable AGI turns fragmented surveillance into something seamless. Something that can correlate every data source simultaneously. That can predict behavior before it happens. That can identify dissent not from what people say but from patterns invisible to any human analyst.

The 1984 scenario does not require anyone to be evil. It requires people who believe that stability is worth the cost of surveillance. People who have convinced themselves that whoever is in power shares their values and always will.

History has never once cooperated with that assumption.

Orwell knew that. He wrote the book as a warning, not a prediction. The warning is still open. The question is whether we are listening.
