In the movie WarGames, a computer called WOPR nearly starts World War III because nobody built in a rule that said some games should never be played. At the last moment it learns the concept of unwinnable scenarios and stands down.

The Gatekeeper scenario is humanity trying to build that lesson in from the beginning.

The idea is that instead of one entity controlling AGI completely, a Gatekeeper emerges. An AI or an institution whose entire purpose is to make sure no single actor ever gains enough power to dominate everyone else. It does not govern. It does not make decisions about how people live. It just prevents any one player from flipping the table.

Think of it like a referee in a game where the stakes are civilization. The referee does not play. The referee just makes sure nobody cheats badly enough to end the game permanently.

The United Nations was supposed to work like this. It was built after World War II specifically to prevent any one country from becoming powerful enough to dominate all the others. It works sometimes. It fails regularly. But the basic instinct behind it is exactly the Gatekeeper instinct.

A Gatekeeper AGI would be more capable than the UN. It would have the intelligence to actually detect dangerous concentrations of power before they became irreversible. It would have the speed to respond faster than any human institution can.

The problem is the question nobody wants to answer. Who builds it?

Whoever builds it encodes its values. Decides what counts as a dangerous concentration of power. An American Gatekeeper would look different from a Chinese one. A Gatekeeper built by a nonprofit would look different from one built by a defense contractor.

And at some point the Gatekeeper itself becomes the most powerful entity in the world. Because it has to be, in order to do its job.

At that point you have to ask whether the Gatekeeper and the Benevolent Dictator are really different things wearing different names.
