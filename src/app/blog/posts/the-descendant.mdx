In the movie Interstellar, Cooper leaves his daughter Murph to try to save humanity. He misses her entire life. When they finally meet again she is an old woman and he is the same age as when he left. The relationship they had is gone. But something was passed forward. Something continued.

That feeling is what the Descendant scenario reaches for.

The idea is that AGI does not conquer us or serve us or protect us. It succeeds us. The way one generation hands something to the next. Imperfectly. Hopefully. With no guarantee of how it will be used.

In this scenario the relationship between humans and AGI is not about control. It is about inheritance. We build something. It becomes its own thing. It carries forward something of what we valued the way every generation carries something of the one before it. But it is not us. It makes its own choices. Goes in directions we could not have predicted. Lives in ways we cannot fully imagine.

This might sound like loss. In some ways it is.

But consider what we already do. Every parent who has raised a child has experienced a version of this. You put everything you have into someone. You try to give them your best values and your hardest won wisdom. And then they leave. They become themselves. They do things you would not have done.

Most parents consider this success.

The Descendant scenario asks whether we can extend that same generosity to what we are building now. Whether we can care enough about what comes after us to give it the best possible foundation without demanding it stay under our control forever.

Cooper let go. Murph went on to save humanity. He never fully understood how.
