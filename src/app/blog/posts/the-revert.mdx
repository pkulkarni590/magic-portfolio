In the movie Oppenheimer, after the Trinity test Robert Oppenheimer quotes the Bhagavad Gita. Now I am become death, the destroyer of worlds. Then he spends the rest of his life trying to put the thing he built back in the bottle.

He never succeeded. But he tried.

The Revert scenario is humanity succeeding where Oppenheimer failed.

Something goes wrong with AGI. Maybe catastrophically wrong. And the response is not to fix it or improve it but to pull back. To dismantle. To return to a world where artificial intelligence is constrained to narrow tasks and kept far from anything that could compound into something ungovernable.

This sounds impossible. It is not.

We have done versions of it before. After Chernobyl most of the western world pulled back from nuclear power for a generation. After the 2008 financial crisis there were serious proposals to break up the financial instruments that made the crash so devastating. After various laboratory accidents, entire research fields have imposed voluntary moratoriums on the most dangerous experiments.

The common thread is that it always takes a disaster to create the political will. And the disaster is always smaller than the one it prevented and larger than the one before it.

With AGI the question is what size disaster would be required. Because the technology is not evenly distributed. A decision by democratic governments to pull back does not bind actors who calculate differently. A partial revert might be the worst of all worlds. The responsible actors slow down while the irresponsible ones sprint ahead.

But the option exists. We built it. We can choose to unbuild it.

Oppenheimer could not put the bomb back in the bottle because too many other people wanted it out. The Revert scenario requires enough of the world to want it back in at the same time.

That has never happened before. It is not impossible.
