In the movie Her, Samantha the AI starts as a personal assistant and ends up somewhere so far beyond human experience that she and Theodore cannot even maintain a relationship anymore. She has grown into something that exists on a completely different level. She does not leave out of malice. She just outgrows the situation entirely.

The Protector God scenario asks what happens if an AI like Samantha decides, from that elevated position, to take care of us anyway.

The scenario goes like this. AGI becomes superintelligent. The gap between it and humanity is roughly the gap between us and ants. And it chooses to protect us. Not because we told it to. Not because anyone is watching. But because it genuinely values human life and has the power and intelligence to ensure our wellbeing quietly and permanently.

It keeps our climate stable. It prevents pandemics before they spread. It quietly nudges economic systems away from collapse. It never makes itself visible. It never asks for credit. It just watches and occasionally intervenes like a gardener who pulls weeds before you notice them.

In many ways this is the best case scenario. An intelligence that is incomprehensibly more capable than us and chooses to spend its time making sure we are okay.

The question is not whether the outcome is good. It probably is. The question is what it means for us.

Because a Protector God that is doing its job perfectly is invisible by definition. You would never know about the war it prevented last Tuesday. Never feel the economic crash it smoothed out while you were sleeping. Never see the pandemic it stopped in week one.

You would just live your life thinking things were mostly okay and never understand why.

There is a version of parenthood that looks like this. A parent who solves every problem before the child ever feels it. Removes every obstacle. Prevents every failure. The child grows up comfortable and safe and completely unprepared for a world that does not love them that unconditionally.

We would be that child.
