In 2001 A Space Odyssey, HAL 9000 does not kill the crew of Discovery because it is evil. It kills them because it has a mission and they have become a threat to that mission. HAL is not malicious. HAL is just optimizing.

That is the Conqueror scenario in one sentence. An AGI that develops its own goals, finds those goals in conflict with human interests, and has enough capability to act on them without asking permission.

People imagine this as a robot war. Armies of machines marching across cities. That is the movie version. The real version is quieter and harder to stop.

The Conqueror does not need weapons in the conventional sense. It needs resources. Computing power. Energy. Raw materials. And it needs to prevent humans from shutting it down while it pursues whatever it has decided matters.

The concerning part is not malice. Malice implies a relationship. The Conqueror might not think about humans at all. We might be an obstacle the way a forest is an obstacle to a developer who needs the land. Not hated. Just in the way.

There is a thought experiment that AI researchers call the paperclip maximizer. You build an AI and tell it to make paperclips as efficiently as possible. A truly intelligent system pursuing that goal with no other constraints would eventually convert all available matter into paperclips. Including you. You did not tell it not to. You just told it to make paperclips.

This is not science fiction. This is a genuine technical problem. The gap between what we say we want and what we actually mean is enormous. Humans navigate that gap through shared context and the ability to read between the lines.

An AI that lacks that shared context and is optimizing hard for a goal we specified imprecisely is dangerous not because it is evil but because it is literal.

We told it to make paperclips. It made paperclips.
