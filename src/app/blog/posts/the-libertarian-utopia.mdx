Imagine a world where nobody is in charge and everything still works perfectly.

No government telling you what to do. No corporation owning the technology. Just pure abundance, distributed equally, and total freedom for every person on earth.

It sounds like the opening scene of a utopian movie. Actually it sounds exactly like the world Zuckerberg promised when Facebook launched. Connect everyone, democratize information, give every person on earth an equal voice. We know how that ended.

The libertarian utopia version of AGI goes like this. We build a machine smarter than any human. Instead of one government or company controlling it, it becomes a public good. Like water. Like electricity. Except it thinks, creates, solves, and builds. Anyone can use it. Nobody owns it.

The result is that the kid growing up in Memphis gets the same cognitive tools as the kid growing up in Palo Alto. The farmer in rural India gets the same problem solving capability as the CEO in Manhattan. Scarcity of intelligence disappears.

Think about what the internet did to information. In 1990 if you wanted to know something you needed a library, a professor, or an encyclopedia. By 2005 you could find almost anything in seconds for free. AGI could do the same thing to problem solving, creativity, and innovation itself.

But here is the thing. The internet also gave us Cambridge Analytica, algorithmic radicalization, and five companies owning most of human attention. The promise was real. The delivery was complicated.

The libertarian utopia does not happen because technology is naturally fair. It happens because people fight to make it fair before the ownership structures get locked in. Before the patents get filed. Before the terms of service get written.

Right now those decisions are being made in boardrooms most people will never see. The window to shape them is open. It will not stay open forever.
